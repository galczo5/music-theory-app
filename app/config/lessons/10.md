## What are Diatonic Chords?

Diatonic chords are the chords that "naturally" belong to a specific scale. You can build a chord starting on every note of the scale.

## The "1-3-5" Rule

To build a basic triad from a scale, you simply skip every other note:

1.  **Pick a starting note** (The Root).
2.  **Skip** the next note and take the **Third**.
3.  **Skip** the next note and take the **Fifth**.

---

## Example: The C Major Scale

Let's use the notes: **C - D - E - F - G - A - B**

- **Chord I:** Start on C -> Skip D -> take E -> Skip F -> take G.
  - Notes: **C - E - G** (C Major)
- **Chord II:** Start on D -> Skip E -> take F -> Skip G -> take A.
  - Notes: **D - F - A** (D Minor)

---

## The Major Scale Pattern

Every Major scale follows the same pattern of chord types. We use Roman Numerals to name them:

| Degree   | Chord Type | Example in C Major |
| :------- | :--------- | :----------------- |
| **I**    | Major      | C                  |
| **ii**   | Minor      | Dm                 |
| **iii**  | Minor      | Em                 |
| **IV**   | Major      | F                  |
| **V**    | Major      | G                  |
| **vi**   | Minor      | Am                 |
| **vii°** | Diminished | Bdim               |

---

## Why is this useful?

- It helps you know which chords "sound good" together.
- Most songs are written using chords from the same scale.
- If you know the scale of a song, you can easily guess the chords!

**Tip:** Notice that chords I, IV, and V are always Major, while ii, iii, and vi are always Minor.
