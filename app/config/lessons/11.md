## What is a Chord Progression?

A chord progression is simply a sequence of chords played one after another. Most of your favorite songs are built using just 3 or 4 chords from the same scale.

## The Roman Numeral System

To talk about progressions, we use the Roman Numerals from the previous lesson. This allows us to play the same "pattern" in any key.

- **Uppercase (I, IV, V):** Major chords.
- **Lowercase (ii, iii, vi):** Minor chords.

---

## Common Progressions to Know

### 1. The "Rock & Roll" (I - IV - V)

This is the most famous progression in history.

- **In the key of C:** C - F - G
- **Sound:** Energetic and strong.

### 2. The "Pop" Progression (I - V - vi - IV)

Used in hundreds of famous hits.

- **In the key of C:** C - G - Am - F
- **Sound:** Catchy and modern.

### 3. The "50s" Progression (I - vi - IV - V)

Common in older "Doo-wop" songs and love ballads.

- **In the key of C:** C - Am - F - G
- **Sound:** Nostalgic and sweet.

### 4. The "Jazz Start" (ii - V - I)

The foundation of Jazz and many pop standards.

- **In the key of C:** Dm - G - C
- **Sound:** Smooth and professional.

---

## How to Practice

1.  Choose a key (like C Major).
2.  Pick a progression from the list above.
3.  Play each chord for 4 beats.
4.  Try to switch between them smoothly!

---

**Tip:** Once you learn a progression in one key, try "transposing" it. For example, play a I-IV-V in the key of G Major (G - C - D).
